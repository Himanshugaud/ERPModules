# CONSTRUCTION ERP BUSINESS OFFERINGS SHOWCASE

## Business Website and Sales Experience Master Prompt

Create a polished, credible, conversion-focused showcase for a dedicated cloud ERP solution built for small and medium-scale construction, contracting, manufacturing, and project-based companies.

The showcase must help a business owner quickly understand:

* What business problems the ERP solves
* Which capabilities are available
* How a dedicated environment protects and isolates company data
* What each subscription plan includes
* What implementation, migration, training, support, and customization cost
* What the next step is to evaluate or purchase the solution

This is not a generic software landing page. It is a practical buying guide for owners, directors, operations heads, project managers, procurement leaders, and finance decision-makers.

Do not use fake customer logos, fabricated testimonials, invented certifications, unsupported uptime claims, or features that are not identified as currently available.

---

# 1. PRODUCT POSITIONING

Position the product as:

> A dedicated construction and project operations ERP that connects projects, people, materials, inventory, and production in one secure company environment.

Primary value proposition:

* Replace disconnected spreadsheets and manual registers
* Give owners one view of projects, stock, employees, clients, and production
* Track materials from receipt through warehouse, site issue, transfer, and consumption
* Improve accountability with roles, permissions, status workflows, and audit-ready records
* Configure the solution around the customer's operating process
* Keep each customer's application and database in an independently managed Azure environment

Do not position the product as a complete replacement for every accounting, payroll, CRM, or enterprise ERP product. Present integrations and additional modules as scoped implementation options unless they are explicitly available.

---

# 2. TARGET CUSTOMERS

Design the offering for organizations with approximately 10 to 150 users, including:

* General contractors
* Civil and infrastructure contractors
* Interior and fit-out companies
* Engineering and fabrication businesses
* Project-based manufacturers
* Real-estate project teams
* Material suppliers with warehouses and production operations

Show common warning signs that indicate a good fit:

* Project information is spread across spreadsheets and messaging applications
* Management cannot see current stock or material movement
* Sites maintain separate, inconsistent records
* Purchase and material decisions are difficult to trace
* Project ownership and task accountability are unclear
* Reports take days to compile
* Existing enterprise ERP products are too expensive or too rigid

# 5. DEDICATED CUSTOMER ENVIRONMENT

Explain dedicated hosting in business language.

Each managed customer receives an independently configured Azure environment containing:

* A customer-specific web application
* A customer-specific backend API
* A separate Azure SQL logical server and database
* Separate storage, secrets, monitoring, and backups
* A customer-specific domain or subdomain
* Independent deployment, scaling, data export, and restore capability

State that this is logical single-customer isolation on Microsoft Azure. It is not a claim of dedicated physical hardware.

Offer two commercial hosting choices:

## Managed Dedicated Cloud

The provider owns and operates the Azure resources. Hosting, monitoring, backups, upgrades, and support are included within defined plan limits.

## Customer-Owned Azure

Resources are deployed into the customer's Azure subscription. The customer pays Microsoft directly, while software licensing, deployment, maintenance, and support are invoiced separately.

Explain that customer-owned Azure is suitable for companies with internal IT governance or specific compliance requirements.

# 14. CONTENT AND COMPLIANCE RULES

* Use direct business language and define unavoidable technical terms.
* Never claim that dedicated logical Azure resources are dedicated physical hardware.
* Never claim certifications, regulatory compliance, SLAs, or security guarantees that have not been formally established.
* Never show unimplemented roadmap features as currently available.
* Keep recurring subscription, included infrastructure, excess infrastructure, and one-time implementation charges separate.
* Display all prices consistently and state whether GST is included.
* Treat customer data ownership, export, retention, deletion, backup, and custom IP ownership as contractual topics.
* Recommend legal review of the master service agreement, privacy policy, data-processing terms, support policy, and SLA before commercial launch.

The finished showcase should leave a business owner able to answer four questions without speaking to sales:

1. Is this ERP suitable for my company?
2. What operational problems will it solve?
3. What will the subscription and implementation approximately cost?
4. What happens after I request a demonstration?
